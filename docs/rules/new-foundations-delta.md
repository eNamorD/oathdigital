# New Foundations delta from base Oath

This file records explicit change statements in the Welcome booklet. It is not a substitute for the complete combined rulebook.

## Expansion boundary and physical migration

- New Foundations is all-or-nothing; its elements are not meant to be introduced piecemeal. A fresh campaign is recommended, though conversion guidance exists externally (NF p. 1).
- Remove old walkthrough setup cards and 36 listed base denizens; most receive replacements with matching IDs, while three named cards become edifices. Replace all player/Chancellor boards, both banners, all Oathkeeper goals/title, Reliquary board, all edifices, Visions, relics (including Grand Scepter), and sites (NF pp. 2-4).
- Atlas replaces World box. It stores all 24 sites. Each of six Homelands is paired with one random ruined matching-suit edifice; remaining edifices, setup/foundation cards, legacies, Chronicle tasks, denizens, and dividers are organized as specified (NF pp. 5-6).
- New components include two more player colors, first-player and Usurper-limiter markers, 36 legacies, 11 setup cards (6 foundations), 5 Chronicle tasks, 24 physical bandit warbands, and revised favor/secret token denominations (NF pp. 3, 6).

## Campaign structure

- **Era** is new: winners score lineage points across a group-defined span; highest total wins the era. Ending an era resets only points, not world state (NF p. 7).
- First game is randomized rather than fixed walkthrough setup; all players start Exiles, all sites are faceup, Homelands include ruined edifices, sites have physical bandits, players begin with a dormant legacy, and no one initially has Oathkeeper (NF p. 8).
- The Chronicle is redistributed across task holders rather than controlled wholly by the winner. Legacy activation/scoring and six mutable foundation cards create persistent changes (NF p. 9).

## Map and pieces

- All sites were replaced. Sites add Recover Difficulty; site powers changed; Homeland and Enduring are new concepts, and dual-power sites exist (NF pp. 10-11).
- Bandits are now warbands that physically rule empty sites and refill after an action. They do not compete for victory goals (NF p. 11).
- Site starting resources require the built-in Wake power Take Wealth and cannot be taken if an enemy pawn is present (NF p. 11).
- Atlas preserves the geography of both recent and forgotten sites across games and eras (NF p. 12).

## Banners

- Banners always contribute three defense dice when targeted, independent of stored resources.
- Adding resources is now a banner minor action, not Recover.
- Both are double-sided; foundations select the active face.
- Mob's discard permission applies to the played-card site, not any site in the region, and no longer forces Wake favor placement.
- Wandering Flame can move its holder to any secret-bearing site as a non-Travel minor action and can place a secret on the holder's site (NF p. 12; full rules CR p. 32).

## Actions

- Campaign is split into **Conquest** (sites) and **Raid** (mandatory co-located enemy pawn plus any number, including zero, of its faceup relics/banners); one campaign cannot target both categories. Raid no longer targets the pawn's site, uses the defender's board warbands as its force, and discards all facedown adviser/relic cards on success (NF p. 13; complete order CR pp. 22-23).
- Attacker sacrifices occur before defense roll. Facedown battle plans can be revealed during campaign and can still be lost to a successful raid (NF p. 13).
- Muster yields 1 warband plus one per matching faceup adviser, instead of a flat 2 (NF p. 13).
- Trading for secrets places one favor and burns one favor, instead of placing two favor; yield is unchanged (NF p. 13).
- **Forge** is new and converts site-printed resources on three empty denizens into a facedown relic (NF p. 14).
- Recover only takes relics, uses repeated two-defense-die rolls at 1 Supply each, and checks site Recover Difficulty (NF p. 14).
- Facedown relics are new: powerless while hidden, but count for goals and are vulnerable to raid (NF p. 14).
- **Challenge** is new and exclusively handles taking banners through resource comparison/replacement (NF p. 15).
- Negotiation is an always-available, co-location minor action for consensual favor/relic exchange. Citizenship offers occur through it and no longer require a Reliquary relic (NF p. 15).

## Empire and other changes

- Exiling another Citizen requires the Grand Scepter holder to win a campaign and pay its battle-plan cost; self-exile pays Chancellor favor equal to board warbands, minimum 3 (NF p. 15).
- Edifices are denizens; ruined edifices no longer block Trade/Muster (NF p. 16).
- Protection/Sanctuary count relics only, not banners (NF p. 16).
- Reliquary is a pile behind a tile; Grand Scepter holder chooses one relic from it during later-game setup (NF p. 16).
- **Bury** routes denizen/relic/edifice to the bottom of its corresponding deck and ignores lock (NF p. 16).
- Map-order vocabulary Hinterward, Cradleward, Cradlemost, and Hintermost is new (NF p. 16).
- Conspiracy now directly takes a relic/banner from a co-located enemy, then returns to box; it can be discarded by raid (NF p. 16).
- "Enemies cannot" persistent powers require timely enforcement; forgetting grants consent for that event (NF p. 16).
- Empire rules were simplified; use CR pp. 34-35 for the normative version (NF p. 16).
